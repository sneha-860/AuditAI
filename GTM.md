# GTM

## Target User

The target user is not a generic startup founder. It is an Engineering Manager at a 15-40 person Series A SaaS startup who manages the AWS/tooling budget and gets quarterly "cut costs" pressure from their CFO. They know developers are using Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and direct API spend, but they do not have a simple way to defend which tools are necessary and which are duplicate spend.

Their pain shows up during budget review: finance sees several AI subscriptions, engineering says the tools improve output, and nobody has a clean savings estimate. When the manager searches, they use practical comparison and justification queries:

- `cursor vs github copilot cost`
- `is claude pro worth it for team`
- `reduce ai tool spend startup`
- `ai tool budget justification`

## Where They Already Spend Attention

Credex should meet this user where engineering leaders already debate tooling. The highest-signal communities are `r/ExperiencedDevs`, `r/EngineeringManagement`, Rands Leadership Slack in the `#tools` channel, Hacker News threads like "Ask HN: How do you manage AI tool budget?", Lenny's Newsletter Slack, and Y Combinator's Bookface if Credex has access through founders or portfolio companies.

The positioning should be concrete: "I built a calculator to find duplicate AI subscriptions" beats "optimize your AI stack." This user will ignore broad SaaS claims, but they will try a useful tool that helps them answer a CFO question in 60 seconds.

## 30-Day $0 Launch Plan

Days 1-3: Post "I built an AI spend calculator" on `r/SideProject` and `r/ExperiencedDevs`. Link to the tool, ask for feedback, and frame the story around a team realizing it was paying for three coding assistants at once. Do not pitch Credex credits in the post.

Days 4-7: Find five Hacker News "Ask HN: tools" or tooling-budget threads from the past six months. Comment genuinely with patterns like duplicate coding assistants, unused seats, and overbought team plans. Mention the tool only where it is directly relevant.

Days 8-14: Cold DM 20 engineering managers on LinkedIn with: "I built this after our team realized we were paying for 3 coding assistants. It gives a 60-second AI spend audit and does not ask for email until after results. Would love to know if the recommendations feel accurate for a 20-person eng team." Track replies, audits, and objections in a spreadsheet.

Days 15-21: Publish "We audited 50 startups' AI spend - here's what we found." Use early data: average monthly spend, most common redundant pair, unused seats, and percent of teams already spending well. Share it back to Reddit, HN, LinkedIn, and relevant Slack communities.

Days 22-30: Submit to BetaList, IndieHackers, and Product Hunt as a soft launch. The Product Hunt angle should be "free AI spend audit for engineering teams."

## Unfair Distribution Channel

Credex already has relationships with companies overpaying for AI credits. Email existing prospects: "Before you buy credits, run this free audit so we can separate subscription waste from usage spend." These warm leads are already cost-conscious and more likely to convert than anonymous public traffic.

## Week 1 Traction Target

If it works, Week 1 should produce 200 completed audits, 40 captured emails at 20% conversion, 5 consultation bookings from high-value audits, and $0 spent. The product-learning target is 15 useful comments or replies naming missing tools, confusing recommendations, or pricing assumptions to tighten.
